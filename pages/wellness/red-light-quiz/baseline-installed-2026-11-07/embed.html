The science behind the light
There’s a reason we’re interested.

  
Skin appearance
Encouraging results for skin appearance
A 2025 randomized LED-mask study reported improvements in some wrinkle measurements and satisfaction after four weeks of treatment. Read the facial rejuvenation trial.
Study details and how they relate to Matrix
The study found no improvement in its Wrinkle Assessment Scale. It compared two with three weekly treatments and found no significant extra benefit from more sessions on reported satisfaction and photographic outcomes. It used an LED mask, not a BioMax panel, so it does not validate a specific Matrix dose or guarantee an outcome.

  
Pattern hair loss
A longer view of hair support
A review of 38 studies and 3,098 participants found significantly improved hair density versus placebo for pattern hair loss. That is worth a closer look when thinning is your priority. Read the alopecia review.
Study context & choosing the right next step
Results varied and do not establish equivalent outcomes for a BioMax panel. Sudden, patchy, painful, or inflamed hair loss needs medical assessment. A first scalp-focused visit must confirm appropriate scalp coverage before any course is considered.

  
Comfort & movement
Promising findings for joint and muscle comfort
A knee osteoarthritis review found lower pain at rest compared with placebo. A separate muscle-recovery review found reduced soreness with PBM applied before exercise. Read the joint review and muscle-recovery review.
How we use this research
The knee finding was very low-certainty evidence and the muscle-soreness finding was low-certainty. Devices, doses, and applications differ. These studies support continued interest, not guaranteed relief or a validated dose for this panel.
Whole-body results differ from localized sports protocols; a five-study review found no benefit for fatigue biomarkers or exercise performance. Read the whole-body review.

  
Tissue support
Light is being studied in tissue repair, too
Some trials in a six-study review reported better wound healing or wound-bed quality when LED therapy was added to standard care. Read the tissue-healing review.
Medical research & spa care are different
Findings were inconsistent and certainty was very low. Matrix does not provide wound care; open wounds and postoperative restrictions stay with the treating clinician. Your report can help you understand the research without replacing that care.

 

 

  
Before you book
A few things worth knowing

  
How often would I come in?
Your answers shape a starting rhythm and a review point. Try one visit, build a consistent routine, or revisit an established one. There is no single series that everyone has to buy.

  
Is this the same as a red light hair-loss device?
No. Research on scalp devices is encouraging for assessed pattern hair loss, but it doesn’t establish the same results with a full-body panel. If hair loss is sudden, patchy, painful, or inflamed, a medical assessment comes first. Read the hair-loss review.

  
Do I need to buy supplements?
No. If you ask for nutrition notes, they’ll be based on the food-intake gaps or existing clinician recommendation you report. You can skip them entirely. Any personalized supplement plan is a separate conversation, not a condition of booking.
