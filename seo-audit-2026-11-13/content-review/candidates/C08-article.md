# Massage, Red Light and Nerve Discomfort: Setting Useful Goals

Burning, tingling, numbness and radiating pain can make everyday tasks difficult. They can also arise for different reasons. Finding the cause is the first step; a wellness service should not be presented as a diagnosis or a cure for nerve damage.

## Begin with medical guidance

Discuss new, persistent or worsening nerve symptoms with a healthcare professional. Ask which activities and types of touch are appropriate, whether there are areas to avoid and what changes should prompt further assessment. Bring those instructions to a bodywork appointment.

## What massage may offer

When appropriate for your condition, massage can focus on comfort, relaxation and surrounding muscle tension. A chronic low-back pain trial reported benefits for symptoms and function, but that finding does not demonstrate nerve regrowth or apply to every neuropathy or sciatica diagnosis.

Reduced discomfort during a session should not be interpreted as proof that damaged nerves have healed. The goal and the way progress is assessed should be clear from the start.

## Pressure and technique should fit the person

More pressure is not automatically more useful. Tell your therapist about reduced sensation and any area where feedback may be unreliable. ## Supplements need an individual reason

B12 deficiency can affect nerve function, while excess supplemental B6 can cause nerve problems. That makes individualized assessment more useful than a standard combination of B vitamins, alpha-lipoic acid and other products. Review supplements and doses with your clinician or pharmacist.
